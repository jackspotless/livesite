House Cleaning in Scottsdale, AZ

Scottsdale is a lot of different cities under one name — 1950s block ranches in the south, lake communities in the middle, desert custom homes up north. We clean all of it.

Cleaning Scottsdale from the inside out

Scottsdale runs more than 30 miles from the Tempe border to the desert north of Pinnacle Peak, and the housing changes a lot along the way. South Scottsdale is mostly post-war block ranches on quarter-acre lots. The middle of the city is master-planned communities from the 1970s through the '90s, built around lakes, greenbelts, and golf. North of the 101, it's high desert, gated communities, and custom homes designed around the view.

That range is why a one-size checklist doesn't work here. A 1958 ranch with original terrazzo needs different care than a new desert contemporary with honed limestone and a wall of glass, and both need different care than a lock-and-leave condo in Old Town. We clean each home for what it actually is.

Neighborhoods we work in across Scottsdale

We clean from the Tempe line all the way north to Pinnacle Peak. Some of the areas we work in most:

  • Old Town & downtown — Condos, townhomes, and the older single-family streets around the arts district. Smaller footprints, lots of glass, and plenty of second homes.
  • South Scottsdale — The 1950s and '60s block ranch homes south of Camelback Road, many of them remodeled. Saltillo tile and original terrazzo still turn up next to brand-new kitchens.
  • McCormick Ranch — Scottsdale's first master-planned community, built around lakes, two golf courses, and the Indian Bend Wash greenbelt.
  • Gainey Ranch — Guard-gated golf community with patio homes, townhomes, and custom estates on the lakes and fairways.
  • Scottsdale Ranch — Lake-centered neighborhoods east of McCormick Ranch, mostly family homes from the 1980s and '90s.
  • Kierland — The 1990s master plan around Kierland Commons and Scottsdale Quarter, where condos and mid-size family homes sit right on the Phoenix line.
  • Grayhawk & DC Ranch — The big North Scottsdale master plans just above the 101, from production homes and townhomes to custom estates in Silverleaf.
  • Troon & Pinnacle Peak — High-desert homes among the boulders, many of them custom, with long views and plenty of glass.

What's particular about cleaning a Scottsdale home

Hard water shows up on everything glass

Valley water is hard, and Scottsdale homes tend to have a lot of glass — frameless shower enclosures, sliding doors to the patio, glass railings. Mineral spotting builds on all of it. Regular cleaning keeps it from setting into the kind of haze that only comes off with heavy scrubbing. If yours has already set, a deep clean is the right first visit.

Indoor-outdoor living brings the outdoors in

Pools, patios, and desert landscaping are standard here. So is the fine dust that rides in on feet, dogs, and every open slider. Floors near the back door and the baseboards along them get dirty faster than the rest of the house, and we treat them that way.

Monsoon season resets the clock

From July through September, a single dust storm can coat window sills, ceiling fans, and every horizontal surface in a day. Plenty of Scottsdale clients add a one-time clean after a bad storm rather than waiting for their next recurring visit.

Event season means guests

Winter and spring are busy in Scottsdale — Barrett-Jackson in January, the WM Phoenix Open in February, spring training in March. If you're hosting family or friends for any of it, book the pre-arrival clean early. Those weeks fill up.

How often Scottsdale homeowners book cleaning

Bi-weekly is the most common schedule for full-time Scottsdale households, especially families in McCormick Ranch and Scottsdale Ranch. Condo and townhome owners around Old Town often go monthly. Second-home owners tend to book a deep clean before they arrive for the season and a lighter clean before they leave, with nothing in between. Weekly is common for larger homes and busy households where once every two weeks just isn't enough.

Scottsdale — questions

Frequently asked

Do you cover north Scottsdale?

Yes. We clean across Scottsdale, including Grayhawk, DC Ranch, Silverleaf, Troon, and the Pinnacle Peak area. Most of those communities have their own page on this site with neighborhood-specific details.

Do you clean natural stone and specialty finishes?

Yes. We use pH-neutral cleaners on travertine, marble, limestone, and other natural stone, and never vinegar or acidic products. If your installer recommended a specific product, leave it out and we'll use it.

Can you get hard water spots off my shower glass?

Light to moderate spotting comes off during a normal clean. Heavy, long-set mineral buildup takes more time, so we recommend a deep clean for the first visit and recurring cleans after that to keep it from coming back.

Do you clean condos and townhomes in Old Town?

Yes. Smaller homes are a great fit for our 3-Hour Focus Clean, or a standard clean priced by size through the instant quote.

Can you clean before guests arrive for spring training or the Open?

Yes. Pre-arrival cleans are common in January through March. Book a week or two ahead during event weeks, when schedules fill quickly.
